Yo Bro Yo Girl Youth Initiative · Three years of service

I helped build a place to belong.

At Yo Bro Yo Girl, I have worked where coaching, mentorship, and prevention meet. I helped deliver four month-long summer camps and four week-long spring-break camps built around educating youth about gang involvement and creating a safer environment for them to grow.

← Back to my experience

The goal was bigger than keeping young people busy.

A safe environment is built through repetition: familiar adults, clear expectations, activities that give people a reason to show up, and conversations that treat young people with honesty and respect. My role was to help make that structure real. I coached wrestling, supported sports and public-speaking programming, and helped create the kind of routine where youth could participate, ask questions, and be seen.

The camps gave that work a larger rhythm. Across four month-long summer camps and four week-long spring-break camps, I helped support programming that addressed the pressures surrounding gang involvement while giving youth a constructive place to spend their time. The work taught me that prevention is not one speech or one event; it is the steady work of building belonging before someone goes looking for it somewhere else.

02 / My role

I showed up as a coach, facilitator, and mentor.

Each role gave me another way to make the program stronger and more useful to the youth in it.

Coach

Wrestling practices & sports programming

I coached free wrestling practices and helped lead sports activities that gave youth a positive challenge, a team to join, and an outlet for their energy.

Facilitator

Camp activities & youth engagement

During the camps, I helped keep activities organized and welcoming so participants could move between learning, sport, and conversation with a clear sense of structure.

Mentor

Safety, trust & prevention

I helped support education about gang involvement by contributing to an environment where youth could talk about difficult pressures, learn from adults they trusted, and see other ways to spend their time and build their future.

Communicator

Public-speaking programming

I also helped lead public-speaking programming, giving young people practice expressing themselves clearly and participating confidently in a group.

03 / What I carry forward

Belonging is practical work.

Yo Bro Yo Girl changed how I understand community work. A safe space is not created by a slogan; it is created when young people know where to go, what to expect, and who will be there. Over three years, I learned to contribute through the details—setting up an activity, coaching one more practice, helping a participant find their voice, and returning for the next camp.

That is the standard I bring to everything else I build: notice a problem, make the first useful thing, and keep showing up until it becomes part of the community.
